Users control which handbooks to create, their adopted Practices, and the work
performed using them. Review generated instructions and supporting code, test
scripts within their documented scope, protect sensitive data, and respect the
licenses and terms of any third-party material.

A handbook records working methods. It does not grant access, credentials, or
authority to execute work, modify unrelated files, or publish results. Ordinary
work that uses a handbook retains its own authorization and host permissions.
Trellis skill instructions describe intended agent behavior. They do not enforce
that behavior or guarantee model compliance.

A script's effects stay inside the workspace unless its Playbook declares what it
reaches outside, and a declaration grants nothing. A script's location does not confine
arbitrary code. Use host enforcement and evaluate the actual program's effects
before relying on it. Host models, tools, services, data handling, and permissions
are separate from Trellis.
